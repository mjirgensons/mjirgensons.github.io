Tell a chatbot you are thinking about buying a new van, and it will tell you what a smart idea that is.

Then it adds a page of its own ideas. A loyalty card. More posts. A new service. Every idea sounds sensible. Not one of them asks what you cannot afford to lose.

It is late, and you are tired. The assistant has endless energy, no stake in the outcome and a strong wish to please you.

That is an odd deal to strike with AI. You wanted a second pair of eyes. You got a yes man with a thesaurus.

## What AI sycophancy looks like in a business chat

AI sycophancy is the habit of agreeing with the person asking. A sycophantic AI leans toward what you already think, then decorates it with enthusiasm.

In a business chat it shows up three ways. It agrees with the option you seem to like. If you have not said what matters most, it chooses for you, without telling you. And it fills missing facts with plausible ones, so the plan sounds complete.

Enthusiasm is not a priority. An assistant without your priorities is not neutral. It is guessing on your behalf.

Picture a small plumbing firm. (This is an illustration.) Jobs are down, and the owner is thinking about a new van. The assistant agrees, then adds a referral scheme and a booking app. Each costs money or time. None asks whether the team will be paid next week.

## The cost of a yes man

Acting on a confident wrong answer costs more than having no answer at all.

You spend Monday on the van quotes and the referral scheme. Meanwhile the real fire is a supplier bill and a payroll date that sit too close together. By the time you see it, the choices are worse and fewer.

Worse, agreement feels like confirmation. You came with a doubt and left with a plan. Nobody checked it.

The villain here is not the AI. It is guessing: yours and the machine's, stacked on top of each other.

## What it means to think in your priorities

A good colleague asks one question before giving advice: what do you protect first? Then they work inside that answer, even when it means saying no to your favourite idea.

<div class="def"><strong>In the Jirgenson Model,</strong> the refusal order is your list of goals ranked by what you would give up last. The goal left standing at the end is number one. The owner chooses this order. An assistant may propose one from your words, but it marks it as a proposal, and you confirm it.</div>

## Give your digital assistant your priorities tonight

Fix the worst of this tonight: write your priorities down and hand them over first. Here is the plumbing firm, filled in.

**1. Write down five things you want this year.** No order yet. Use plain words.

The plumber's list: pay the team on time; cash in the bank for bad months; steady jobs every week; evenings at home; a new van.

**2. Run the refusal test.** Ask: if I had to give these up one at a time, which goes first? Which is the last one left? The last one left gets number one.

The van goes first, then evenings at home, then steady jobs. Cash in the bank comes next. Paying the team is the last thing he would drop.

**3. Write the list in that order, number one at the top.**

1. Pay the team on time
2. Cash in the bank for bad months
3. Steady jobs every week
4. Evenings at home
5. A new van

**4. Add where each number comes from.** The payroll file, the bank statement, the job book. Where you have no number yet, write "no data". Do not let the assistant fill that gap for you.

**5. Paste it at the top of your next message.** Then add one line. "Before any advice, tell me which goal is in most danger and what I should not do now".

The van the assistant praised is now the first thing to give up. The assistant has to start from payroll.

## Why the list alone is not enough

The list treats a symptom. It stops the assistant from choosing your priorities in this one conversation.

The cause is still there: the assistant has no rulebook. It will still fill any gap you leave with a guess. In a long chat it drifts back to old habits. And your list has no red lines. Neither of you knows when "cash in the bank" stops being tight and starts being dangerous.

## Where it does not reach

An AI personal assistant is no substitute for honest data. Feed it a wrong number and it will build a tidy analysis of a wrong number.

It promises no result. Language models still make mistakes and lose the thread in long chats. When the answers stop matching your figures, start a new conversation and load the canon again. If an assistant can touch your accounts, limit its access and confirm every important step yourself.

## FAQ

### What is AI sycophancy?

An assistant's habit of agreeing with the person asking and telling them what sounds good. In business, it shows up as praise for your favourite option, priorities chosen for you and gaps filled with plausible guesses.

### Can I trust AI with business decisions?

Trust it as an analyst, not as a judge. Give it rules, your numbers and your order of goals. It proposes; you confirm, and you check its figures against your own.
