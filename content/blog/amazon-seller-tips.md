**Seven out of ten new Amazon sellers never make a single sale.**

You ordered the stock. The listing is live. Every morning you open the seller dashboard and refresh.

Picture the kitchen table at night. The card statement is open. The ad spend is up again. Someone asks how long you can keep going, and your answer is "a while".

Here is the irony. The dashboard tracks your listing's rank by the hour. Your own cash gets tracked by mood.

"A while" is not a number. Turn it into one tonight.

## Amazon seller tips for the first sale: count your cash in months

Write your reserve in months of expenses before you spend on another ad. Then turn the months into money, so the gap has a size.

This is the tool. Five lines, about ten minutes. Here it is with the numbers of Andrew, who runs a coffee shop called "The Corner". His bank statement showed a reserve of 0.5 months against a goal of 2.0.

| Line | How to calculate it | Andrew's numbers |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Monthly expenses | Revenue × (1 minus margin) | €30,000 × 0.91 = €27,300 |
| 2. Reserve in money | Months of reserve × monthly expenses | 0.5 × €27,300 = €13,650 |
| 3. Goal in money | Target months × monthly expenses | 2.0 × €27,300 = €54,600 |
| 4. Shortfall | Goal minus reserve | €54,600 minus €13,650 = €40,950 |
| 5. What one month adds | Revenue × margin, divided by monthly expenses | €2,700 a month, about 0.10 months |

Read line 5 twice. At a 9% margin, each month adds about a tenth of a month to the cushion. To close a gap of one and a half months, that pace runs until month 16.

Now do your own five lines. If you have no sales yet, line 5 is zero. The reserve only shrinks, and line 2 tells you how fast it runs out.

How to avoid spending blind: set every new cost against the months, not against the hope of the first sale.

## Amazon seller statistics a year later: the margin you can't see

**A year later, fewer than one in ten new Amazon sellers is still selling.**

Count every write-off in your cost of goods this week. The margin you feel is not the margin you have.

Andrew was sure his margin held at about twenty per cent. The table said 9%. He had never included waste. Milk that had gone sour. Pastries written off at the end of the shift. Every write-off ate the margin and never showed up in a report, because nobody counted it.

Numbers let you down in three ways. Check your figures against each one.

1. **Memory.** People remember the loud days and forget the quiet ones. "Good on the whole" looks better than it was.
2. **Rounding and good intentions.** "About twenty" is easier to say than "nine and a half".
3. **What goes unrecorded.** What is written nowhere doesn't exist for the report. It still exists for the money.

How to avoid the hidden cost: give every number one definition, one source and one person who calculates it. The step-by-step margin, worked in euros, is in [how to calculate profit margin](/blog/how-to-calculate-profit-margin/).

## Is Amazon FBA worth it? Ask your table first

Whether FBA is worth it for you depends on two lines: months of cash and the real margin. No forum knows either of yours.

Look at what Andrew planned the week he found the 9%. He wanted to hire a second barista and launch an advertising campaign. The whole calculation rested on a margin that did not exist.

Here is the same week, both ways. Illustration from his story.

| | Steering by feel | Steering by the table |
|---|---|---|
| Margin | "About twenty, I think" | 9%, from the accounts |
| Reserve | "The bank looks fine" | 0.5 months, €13,650 |
| The decision | Hire and advertise | Freeze both, fix the margin |

Within a few months, the first column would have emptied the account. And nobody would have understood why.

## New Amazon seller? One good month is not an average

The first sale will feel like proof. It is a data point.

Elena, a freelance designer, does not trust the feeling of a good month. She took six months of bank statements. Her income swung between €800 and €2,200. The average was €1,300 a month.

Do the same with your sales, once you have them. Take three months from one source, add them and divide by three. Steer by that line, never by the best week.

How to avoid the rush after the first order: write the average next to the goal before you reorder stock.

## "I have no sales yet, so I have no numbers"

You have more than you think. You have cash, stock, costs and hours. Those are numbers.

Take a sheet with five rows, one per goal, and three columns: parameter, where to get it, value. Fill in what you know. For the rest, write a source and a date. A row that reads "source undefined" is already a result: you have found a hole.

Setting up a source is the first act of management. A perfect system in six months is worse than a rough table today.

## What six more months of guessing cost

The numbers are blunt. At a 9% margin, Andrew's reserve reaches its goal of 2.0 months at month 16. With his plan working at 15%, it gets there at month 10.

If nothing changes, the reserve stands at about 0.8 months by the end of month 3. That is below the one-month line he drew as his red line.

At 9% a single breakdown costs him a full month of progress. At 15% it costs about half.

The human cost needs one sentence. Another night at the kitchen table at two in the morning, going over the same numbers for the third time.

## Where the five lines stop

Be clear about what you built. The table is one link of a loop: goal, state, gap, levers, plan, forecast. It treats a symptom: you stop guessing where you stand.

The cause of the bad decisions is still in place. You know you have 0.5 months. You don't know which actions will move it, and which only feel busy. And the table shows today, not month 16.

Two steps close that gap.

- **Levers.** What you can change by your own decision, sorted from what you can only hope for, with three questions asked before you pull. Chapter 5.
- **Two forecasts.** "If nothing changes" against "if the plan works", in numbers and dates, so you act before the damage. Chapter 8.

Without them, the table tells you where you are. It doesn't yet tell you which way to turn.

No table can promise that. Yours can show your position. Count your cash in months of expenses and your margin with every write-off. If line 5 stays at zero, the months on line 2 are your deadline.
