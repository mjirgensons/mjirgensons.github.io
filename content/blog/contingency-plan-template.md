Your car warns you before the engine cooks. Your business waits for the smoke.

The dashboard needle sits in the green, the yellow or the red. The driver doesn't open the bonnet to decide what to do. The colour already told him.

Now look at your own week. Cash is down. The margin slipped. A big order came in late. Which of those is yellow, and which one is red? And what exactly did you plan to do about each?

If the answer lives in your head, you don't have a contingency plan. You have a mood waiting for a bad day.

The usual contingency plan is a thick binder called "What if". It sits on a shelf. When the "if" arrives, nobody opens it, because it never said when to start.

The fix for a plan that starts too late comes from the Jirgenson Model. Put a trigger on every number, set in advance, and tie an action to each zone. It is scientifically proven that the Jirgenson Model works ([doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934)).

## Contingency plan definition and meaning

A contingency plan is a response prepared in advance for something that may happen: if this occurs, we do that. Its whole value is the timing. You decide while your head is clear, not while the phone rings.

That is the usual contingency plan meaning. It has one weak spot. "If this occurs" names an event, and events rarely announce themselves. A supplier doesn't phone to say your margin crossed a line this morning. The margin drifts, a point at a time, and nobody notices the day it became serious.

So the model moves the trigger from the event to the number.

<div class="def"><strong>In the Jirgenson Model,</strong> the gap between the goal and the actual state, called the error, is the main signal that drives decisions. Every number gets three zones, normal, acceptable and emergency, fixed in a calm moment. The zone, not the headline, decides what happens next.</div>

An event is a story. A zone is a reading. You can argue with a story for a week. A reading tells you where you stand in one glance.

## The contingency plan template: five steps, filled in

Here is the whole template. Each step sits next to the entries from the book's coffee shop, run by Andrew. His figures are a worked illustration.

### 1. List the numbers that can hurt you

Keep it short: no more than nine rows. Andrew used five: cash reserve, margin, regular customers, complaints and his own hours.

### 2. Write the goal, today's value and the error

The error is actual minus goal, row by row. The sign shows the direction. For complaints, the error is the count above the goal.

| Number | Goal | Now | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash reserve, months of expenses | 2.0 | 0.5 | −1.5 |
| Margin, % | 15 | 9 | −6 pp |
| Regular customers, % of receipts | 40 | 25 | −15 pp |
| Complaints per week | 2 | 5 | +3 |
| Owner's hours per week | 40 | 65 | +25 |

### 3. Set three thresholds for each row

This is the heart of the template. Three rules decide where the lines go.

- **No finer than your measurement.** If your margin count can be wrong by one point, a green band half a point wide is noise. Improve the measurement instead of narrowing the zone.
- **Red starts where damage gets hard to reverse.** Andrew drew the reserve's red line at one month. His reason: one month is enough time to ask for a deferral, raise money and make a choice. Under a month, those options are gone.
- **Set them calm, change them rarely.** Moving a line mid-crisis bends the ruler to fit the result.

Andrew's thresholds, written the same evening:

| Number | Normal | Acceptable | Emergency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash reserve, months | 2.0 or more | 1.0 to under 2.0 | under 1.0 |
| Margin, % | 14 or more | 5 to under 14 | under 5 |
| Regular customers, % | 36 or more | 20 to under 36 | under 20 |
| Complaints per week | 2 or fewer | 3 to 8 | more than 8 |
| Owner's hours per week | 42 or fewer | over 42, up to 70 | more than 70 |

One refinement for a number that hovers near a line. Use one threshold to enter a zone and another to leave it, or ask for two readings in a row. Otherwise the colour flickers with every bit of noise and the plan fires twice a week for nothing.

### 4. Write the action each zone triggers

This turns a table of colours into a contingency plan. Each zone gets its own standing order.

| Zone | Standing order |
|---|---|
| Normal | Do nothing. Leave a working number alone. |
| Acceptable | Find the cause while it is still cheap. Ask: what changed since last time? Since when? Which lever acts on it? |
| Emergency | Check for a direct threat to people, safety, mandatory payments or the law first. Freeze work that doesn't help this number. Put time, money and attention on it. |
| Undefined | No data, old data or figures that contradict each other. Measure first. Grey is not green. |

The green order is the hardest to obey. It guards against management's most common sin: fixing what works.

### 5. Read the colours and run the orders

Sort today's errors into zones. Andrew's panel at the start:

| Number | Zone | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Cash reserve | Emergency | Everything else waits until it is back above one month |
| Margin | Acceptable | Working explanation: waste and loss-making menu items |
| Regular customers | Acceptable | Cause unknown. His task list got one line: "find out" |
| Complaints | Acceptable | Look for the cause |
| Owner's hours | Acceptable | Look for the cause |

Look at the regular customers row. "Cause unknown" is a result, not a failure. The yellow zone asked him to start looking, and he did.

That is the whole template. It takes about fifteen minutes.

## A contingency plan example from a pocket-money budget

The same table works far from a coffee shop. Alex, a boy saving for a bicycle, planned to have €170 by 1 July. He had €140. The error: −€30.

His thresholds: up to €10 behind is normal, €11 to €40 is acceptable, more than €40 is an emergency. Minus 30 lands in the yellow. No panic. The cause is plain: the scooter he meant to sell hasn't sold yet. So the yellow order applies. He watches the gap and knows which lever closes it.

A contingency plan for a business and one for a bicycle share one skeleton: goal, reading, three zones, one order per zone. Only the units change.

## What "we'll see" costs over six months

Skip the template and the reserve stays at "we'll see".

If nothing changes, Andrew's reserve stands at about 0.8 months by month 3. Still under the red line of one month. A full quarter in the emergency zone, and nobody declared it. At a 9% margin the goal of two months arrives at month 16. With the margin plan working, at month 10.

The margin row shows the money. Six points under target on €30,000 of monthly revenue is €1,800 of profit missing every month. Six months of "we'll see" costs €10,800 against the target.

And the human cost is plain. Without triggers, the owner works on whichever problem rang last, and the red one waits behind it.

"My numbers aren't precise enough for zones" is the fair objection. The first threshold rule answers it. Draw the zones as wide as your measurement allows, and sharpen the measurement later. A rough line tonight beats a perfect one next year.

## Where the template stops

Be clear about what you built. The template tells you when to act and what kind of action each colour demands. That treats the symptom: decisions made in the order problems arrive.

The cause is still in place. In the red, the template says "put everything on this number". It doesn't say which of your moves shifts it, and how fast. And the panel shows today, so the red line still arrives as a surprise.

Two steps close that gap.

- **The list of levers.** It sorts the moves you control from the ones you only hope to control. It also shows how fast each acts. Chapter 5.
- **Two forecasts.** One line for "change nothing", one for "the plan works", each with the month it crosses a threshold. Chapter 8.

The template covers one link of the control loop, the gap. The other links are the goal order, the real state, the levers, the plan and the forecast. A zone without a lever is an alarm with no exit.

## FAQ

### What is a contingency plan?

A response prepared in advance for something that may happen: if this occurs, we do that. A working one ties each response to a number crossing a threshold, so you know the exact moment to start.

### What is a contingency plan example for a small business?

A coffee shop sets its cash reserve red line at one month of expenses. Below it, every new expense that doesn't rebuild the reserve stops. Between one and two months, the owner looks for the cause while it is cheap.

### What should a contingency plan template include?

For each key number: the goal, today's value, the error, three zone thresholds and the action each zone triggers. Add a grey zone for missing data. Fill it in during a calm week and revise it rarely.
