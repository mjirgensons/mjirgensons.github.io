**Half of all creators earn less than a part-time job pays.**

You know the feeling. The analytics page shows a great week. The bank account shows a thin month.

Picture Sunday night. One sponsor paid on time. Another is late. The views from last month's best video have already faded. Ask yourself what you earn, and the honest answer is your best month.

Here is the irony. The analytics page counts every view to the second. Your own pay gets counted by feel.

You can fix that tonight, with one sheet of paper.

## Creator income is a goal row, not your best month

Write your income as a goal with a number, a unit and a deadline. Then measure it as an average, never as your best month.

Elena is a freelance designer who is preparing her first online book. Her bank statements for six months showed income between €800 and €2,200. The average was €1,300 a month.

Her goal reads: at least €3,000 a month, as a three-month average, by month 14.

Check your own goal the same way. It takes one minute.

- "Earn more" has no number.
- "3,000" has no unit.
- "€3,000 a month" has no deadline.
- "€3,000 a month by month 14" is a goal.

How to avoid the best-month trap: take the figure from your bank statements, from the same source every time. One good month does not make an average.

## How much YouTubers make is the wrong first question

**Almost half of full-time creators make less than 1,000 dollars a year.**

Searching for how much YouTubers make gives you someone else's number. Yours is the one that pays your rent.

So cross out every method on your list before you call it a goal. Going full time is a method. So are 100,000 subscribers, daily uploads and one big viral video. Ask of each item: what for?

Andrew, who runs a coffee shop, had eight items on his first list. Three were methods: loyalty cards, a second barista, a new coffee machine. He asked "what for?" of each one. The three methods collapsed into two goals already on the list. That freed a place for the goal he would defend to the end: money in reserve.

A goal answers one question: what do I want to achieve? A method answers another: how will I do it? Write a method into your goals and you commit to one road before you have seen the map.

How to avoid the full-time trap: keep "full time" off the goal list. Write the income row, and let its number tell you when a switch makes sense.

## Write your creator business goal in six fields

This is the tool. It takes about 15 minutes. A goal becomes real once six fields hold an entry.

Here it is, filled in on Elena's riskiest row: how much of her income one client holds.

| Field | What it asks | Elena's entry |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Parameter | What exactly do I measure? | Share of income from her largest client |
| 2. Definition and source | How is it calculated, and from where? | That client's average monthly income divided by her average income, from bank statements |
| 3. Target and deadline | What value, by when? | No more than 30% by month 14 |
| 4. Current value | Where am I today? | 70%: €910 of her €1,300 average |
| 5. Permissible deviation | Where does normal end? | Normal up to and including 33%; acceptable above 33% and up to 50%; emergency above 50% |
| 6. Rhythm and owner | Who looks, and how often? | Elena herself, at her Monday check |

Now read field 4 against field 5. Seventy per cent sits far past the 50% line. Before any new channel or format, this row says where the danger is.

Fill in the same six fields for your income row. Then rank all your rows with the refusal test. Ask: if things got bad, which goal would I give up first?

Elena's order puts income last to go. Her hours go first. While she writes the book, in months 1 to 3, she may work up to 55 hours a week. That is not a lapse. It is a decision made in advance, with an end date.

## Creator earnings: the usual list against the goal list

The difference shows up on the first page. Here is Elena's first draft for the book next to what she wrote after asking what she does with her own hands. Illustration from her story.

| The usual list | The list she could act on |
|---|---|
| More social media | Write three chapters |
| Increase sales | Prepare the book's page |
| Be more visible | Collect a list of the first hundred addresses |

Every item on the left sounds businesslike. Not one of them can be done tomorrow. Every item on the right can.

This is the order of work in the whole model: goal, state, gap, levers, plan, forecast. The goal list comes first, because every later step measures against it.

## "My content creator income depends on the platform, not on me"

Part of it does. The rules of the platforms where Elena promotes her book may change without warning. She writes that down as an outside factor, with its direction and strength. Then she stops arguing with it.

Her levers are all hers. The price of her service packages. Letters to former clients offering a repeat order. Two new channels to find clients. The text of her book's page. The payout rate is weather. The page is the wheel.

## What six more months of guessing cost

Run Elena's numbers forward without the list. Six months at her old average bring €7,800. Her goal asks for €18,000 over the same six months. That is €10,200 short.

Now look at the client row. If that one client leaves, €910 of her €1,300 goes with them. About €390 a month stays.

Andrew's coffee shop shows the same price in time. At a 9% margin his reserve reaches its two-month goal at month 16. With the plan working, at month 10. Six extra months, one breakdown away from trouble.

And the human cost is plain. Fifty hours a week, with every evening going to whoever pays the most.

## Where the goal list stops

Be honest about what you built. The list treats a symptom: you stop measuring yourself by your best month.

The cause is still in place. Elena's move from client work to her own product is not a goal. It is a change of course with a start, a middle and an end. Without written conditions, you quit too early or never quit at all. And a goal at month 14 doesn't tell you what to do this Monday.

Two steps close that gap.

- **Steady mode and manoeuvre.** When to change the plan and when to change the goal, with launch and stop conditions written in advance. Chapter 10.
- **The backward move.** A walk back from the month 14 goal to the one action that starts this week. Chapter 15.

Without them, the list tells you where you are going. It doesn't tell you when to leave the old road.

## FAQ

### What does content creator mean as a business?

A content creator makes videos, posts, podcasts or books and earns from them. As a business it has the same parts as a coffee shop: goals with numbers, a measured state, and levers you can pull yourself.

### How do I work out my real youtuber income?

Take your bank statements, not the analytics page. Add the last three months and divide by three. Elena's six months ranged from €800 to €2,200, with an average of €1,300. That average is your salary as a creator.

### When should I become a full time creator?

When the numbers say so, not the mood. Write the condition before you start, such as an income row and a client row inside their normal lines. The complete method, with launch and stop conditions, is in the full control system.
