Four founders out of five hide their stress. Three out of four never ask for help.

It's your kid's birthday. Candles, noise, a paper hat on your head. You smile for the photo. In your pocket, the bank app says the month closed red. And the word in your head isn't "red". It's "failure".

You'll carry that word through the cake, the drive home and half the night. Nobody at the table will know why you went quiet.

Here is the strange part. You can work. You've kept the doors open, paid people, solved a hundred problems. What breaks you is that every number lands on you with no scale. You carry it all on your back, and your mood does the judging.

The Jirgenson Model gives the answer: a control system that reads a bad month as a gauge, not a sentence. It is scientifically proven that the Jirgenson Model works ([doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934)).

## Why you feel like a failure after one red month

Look at the number that hurt. Minus six points of margin. Is that a lot? A little? Without a line drawn in advance, you decide again every time, usually tired, usually at night.

That's the mechanism. The bad feeling isn't the number. It's the missing scale. A tired brain fills the gap with the worst word it knows.

Picture an engine temperature gauge. Three coloured sectors. Needle in the green: drive on. In the yellow: pay attention. In the red: stop and let it cool. Nobody calls the car a failure when the red light comes on. It's an order to act, and the engine is still in one piece.

<div class="def"><strong>In the Jirgenson Model,</strong> the error is the gap between the goal and the state: actual minus goal, row by row. Three zones, set in advance on a calm day, say how serious each gap is: normal, acceptable and emergency. The needle shows a sector, not a verdict.</div>

The zones are part of the model's public rules, in the [Jirgenson Model canon on GitHub](https://github.com/mjirgensons/jirgenson-canon).

## An instrument reading, not a verdict: the five-step sheet

This is the whole tool. One sheet, about fifteen minutes. Do it for the one number that made you feel like a failure this month.

### 1. Subtract the goal from the actual

Write the goal for today. Write what you have. Subtract.

Alex is thirteen and saving for a bicycle. By his own schedule he should have €170 on 1 July. He has €140. The error is minus €30. One number, no adjectives.

### 2. Name what kind of gap it is

Two different gaps feel the same at night. Keep them apart.

- **Gap to the final goal:** how much of the road remains.
- **Gap to the plan:** where you should be today against where you are.

Andrew's coffee shop holds a reserve of 0.5 months against a goal of 2.0. That's mostly the starting gap, not a slide. And if his plan for month 3 calls for 0.86 and he hits 0.86, the gap to the plan is zero. He hasn't reached the goal. He also hasn't failed at anything. He's on the road, on time. The same reading is laid out row by row in [KPI zones](/blog/kpi-zones/).

### 3. Set three zones while your head is clear

Three rules decide where the lines go:

1. **No tolerance narrower than your measurement.** If your margin figure can be off by a point, a green zone half a point wide is noise.
2. **Red starts where damage gets hard to reverse.** Andrew put red for the reserve below one month. A month gives him time to ask for a deferral, find money and decide.
3. **Write the lines on a calm day and change them rarely.** Moving them mid-crisis bends the yardstick to fit the result. If a number hovers on a line, ask for two readings in a row before the colour changes.

Alex wrote his own: up to €10 behind is normal, €11 to €40 is acceptable, more than €40 is an emergency.

### 4. Read the zone

Now place the gap. Alex at minus €30: yellow. No panic. He knows the cause: the scooter isn't sold yet.

Andrew did the same with five rows on the first evening:

| Goal | Error | Zone |
|---|---|---|
| Cash reserve | −1.5 months | 🔴 Emergency |
| Margin | −6 points | 🟡 Acceptable |
| Regular customers | −15 points | 🟡 Acceptable |
| Complaints | +3 a week | 🟡 Acceptable |
| Owner's hours | +25 a week | 🟡 Acceptable |

One red. Four yellow. Not a failed business. A business with one emergency and four jobs to watch. How to draw the same panel for your own rows is in [traffic light dashboard](/blog/traffic-light-dashboard/).

### 5. Do only the job that zone asks for

Each colour comes with its own action, so you stop arguing with yourself.

- **Green:** leave it alone. Fixing what works is management's most common sin.
- **Yellow:** find the cause while it's cheap. Ask three questions. What changed since last time? Since when? Which lever acts on it?
- **Red:** check for any direct threat to people, payments or the law. Freeze secondary work. Put time, money and attention on the red row. Several reds? First the row nearest to harm that can't be reversed.

That last list is the red protocol. Its first moves, step by step, are in [business crisis: what to do first](/blog/business-crisis-what-to-do-first/).

## Lack of confidence starts with a number nobody calibrated

Andrew's first thought on that evening was loyalty cards. Win the regulars back, right away. A sensible idea.

The sheet said no. Loyalty cards cost money: printing, discounts, setting up the till. That money would come out of a reserve already under a month. Until the red closes, everything else is a luxury.

His senior barista asked what happens if the regulars leave. His answer was calm. Regulars are yellow; they drift away slowly. The reserve is red, and there everything can end fast.

He put the cards aside and, for the first time, felt no guilt. Fatigue didn't make the decision. The order he'd chosen with a clear head did.

That's where confidence at work comes from. Not from a pep talk. From knowing, before the bad night, what you'll do when the needle moves. The order of goals behind that call is in [set priorities with a refusal order](/blog/set-priorities-refusal-order/).

## The family sees the mood, not the number

Your partner can't read your margin. They can read your face. When the number has no scale, home gets the full weight of the word "failure", with no explanation.

So bring the sheet home instead. One sentence works: "The reserve is red, everything else is yellow, and this week I do one thing." That's a fact and an action. It's far lighter to hear than a silence across the dinner table.

One plain line on health. The model treats chaos in the business, not an illness. Does the low mood last more than a bad week or two? Have you lost interest in things you used to enjoy? Then talk to your doctor. In a crisis, in the US or Canada, call or text 988.

## What six more months of verdicts cost

Money first. Andrew's reserve gap was 1.5 months. At expenses of €27,300 a month, that's €40,950 of cushion missing. The margin gap of six points on €30,000 of revenue costs €1,800 of profit a month. Six months of guessing costs €10,800 in profit alone.

Now add the verdict habit. Judged by mood, the loudest problem wins. The loyalty cards get printed, out of a reserve below one month. The red row gets deeper while you fix a yellow one.

And the human cost: six more months of birthdays where you smile for the photo and go quiet in the car.

## Where the zones stop

Be honest about what the sheet does. It treats the symptom. You stop reading your month as a judgment on yourself, and you know which row needs you first.

The cause sits deeper. The zones say where you are and how serious it is. They don't say what you can change. Andrew can't order money to appear. And a red reading today doesn't show where the row will stand in month 10.

Two steps close that gap:

- **Levers.** What you can change today, what you only watch, and which lever acts first. Chapter 5.
- **Two forecasts.** Where the row lands if nothing changes, and if the plan works, with dates. Chapter 8.

Without them, you know which row is red. You don't yet know which handle to pull, or when the red will close. A first look at the handles is in [business levers](/blog/business-levers/).

"Isn't this labelling failure in colours?" No. Red means a line was crossed and nothing irreversible has happened yet. Disaster lies beyond the red zone, and your job is to stay out of it. A red month is an instrument reading, not a verdict.

The zones are the error step of one loop: goal, state, error, levers, plan, forecast.

## FAQ

### Why do I feel like a failure in my business?

Often because a bad number arrives with no scale, so you judge it by mood. Set three zones for each key number on a calm day. Then a red month becomes a reading with one job attached. If the feeling stays for weeks, see a doctor.

### How do I get confidence back after a bad month?

Decide in advance what each colour asks of you. Confidence grows when you know your next move before the needle moves. Write the zones, act on the one red row, and leave the green rows alone.

### How do I overcome self doubt as a business owner?

Separate the gap to the goal from the gap to the plan. You can be far from the goal and exactly on plan. Check the plan line every week, and put your own savings goals on the same scale, as in [save for a goal](/blog/save-for-a-goal/).
