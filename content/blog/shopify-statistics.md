**Two out of three Shopify stores vanish within their first year.**

You built the store in a weekend. The theme looks sharp. The orders don't come.

Picture the evening. You change the banner colour, rewrite a product title, open the analytics again. Three hours later the store looks better. The sales look the same.

Here is the irony. The dashboard reports every click to the second. And the owner still can't say which change moved the money.

That is not a lack of effort. It is effort on things that don't obey you.

Late nights on the store don't move sales when the changes don't obey you. The Jirgenson Model answers that as a control system: real goals, real levers, one early number. It is scientifically proven that the Jirgenson Model works ([doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934)).

## Shopify statistics in year one: list the levers you can pull today

Stop pushing on sales. Move a lever. Shopify statistics count the stores that close; your list counts the levers you hold.

A lever is anything you can change by your own decision. Sales, margin and conversion are results. They move when a lever moves, and never on command.

Here is the whole tool. It takes about ten minutes and one sheet of paper.

**1. Pick one goal in trouble and write every action that comes to mind**. Don't filter yet.
Elena, a freelance designer launching her first online book, wrote: "more social media, increase sales, be more visible".

**2. Ask of each item what exactly you do with your own hands**.
Elena's answers: write three chapters, prepare the book's page, collect a list of the first hundred addresses. Every item sounded less grand. Every item could start tomorrow.

**3. Cross out what depends on other people, the weather, the market or luck**.
Andrew, who runs a coffee shop, crossed out the weather, rent and taxes, and foot traffic in the street. Four lines stayed: prices, menu, purchasing and portion size.

**4. Put three questions to each lever that is left.** Can I move it myself, today? How long before the effect shows? Can I undo it if it doesn't work?
Andrew's answers:

| Lever | Myself, today? | When the effect comes | Rollback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prices | Yes | In weeks; customers take time to adjust | Hard: customers remember the jump |
| Menu | Yes | Quickly | Easy: an item can come back |
| Purchasing | Through negotiation | In a month, after a new contract | Hard: the contract is signed |
| Portion size | Yes | Immediately | Easy, up to the limit where quality suffers |

**5. Start with what is fast and reversible.** Then the slow or irreversible levers. For a store, the range and the product page sit at the top. A long supplier contract sits at the bottom.

**6. Give every financial goal one leading indicator.** A number that moves before the monthly revenue does.

That is the lever list. Andrew read his four lines and felt calmer. Until then he had spent his nerves on everything at once. Now he had four things to tackle the next morning.

<div class="def"><strong>In the Jirgenson Model,</strong> the levers are the parameters you can reach and change by your own decision. Together they form the control vector. What pushes you but does not obey you is an environmental factor: you allow for it, you don't work on it.</div>

## Ecommerce failure rate and the month-two check

**The typical online store lives about six months.**

Six months is not much time to learn which lever works. So set the first check before the money runs out, and set it where the slow levers can show.

Andrew's table explains his timing. Prices take weeks. A new supplier contract takes a month. So his first check came in month 2, with a line written in advance: below 10% margin means revise the plan.

At that check the margin stood at 10.5%. Above the line. Not proof, but a signal the plan was working.

How to avoid the late surprise: on the day you pull a lever, write the check date and the revision line. A check in month five is a post-mortem.

## Ecommerce margin: what one lever buys

Test a lever in money before you pull it hard. Andrew's monthly numbers: revenue €30,000, ingredients €9,000 that rise and fall with customers, other costs €18,300. Profit €2,700. Margin 9%.

Now run one lever on paper. An illustration with his starting numbers: prices up 5%, and 2% of customers leave.

| Line | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | €30,000 | €30,870 |
| Ingredients | €9,000 | €8,820 |
| All expenses | €27,300 | €27,120 |
| Profit | €2,700 | €3,750 |
| Margin | 9% | about 12% |

One lever delivered about 3 points of the 6 he needed. The rise pays as long as fewer than 6.7% of customers leave.

He capped each step at 5%. A bigger jump would have mixed two effects, and he could not have told which caused the drop.

## Etsy statistics and the page that does the selling

**Six out of ten Etsy shops ever opened have gone dark.**

Stop steering the conversion rate. Steer the page.

Elena's book page shows the rule. The share of visitors who buy is a result, not a lever. The lever is what the page says and the order of the blocks on it. She changes the headline and watches how the conversion rate responds.

Her trial stream gave a usable line. Every 100 visitors brought 1.5 buyers. At 1,400 visitors a month that makes 21 sales, about €609 before fees and taxes.

Her leading indicator: 350 page visitors a week and the share of them who buy. Monthly revenue tells her what happened. Weekly visitors tell her what is coming.

How to avoid going dark: give each money goal one leading number, and read it every week. Managing by monthly revenue alone is living by the fire alarm. It rings when the house is already on fire.

## Ecommerce mistakes: pushing on the result

Andrew tried the usual way first. At the morning briefing he told Sara, his senior barista: "We need profit. We have to do something."

"Do what, exactly?" Sara asked.

He did not know. They talked for ten minutes and parted with the feeling of having discussed the weather.

Here are the two meetings side by side. Illustration from his story.

| | The usual Monday | The lever Monday |
|---|---|---|
| The sentence | "We need more sales" | "Prices up 5% in month 2" |
| The task | None | Check the receipts for two weeks |
| The check | Next month's revenue | Margin in month 2, against a 10% line |

Every system works the same way: goal, state, gap, levers, plan, forecast. The levers are the step where talk turns into a task.

## "I have no time for a system"

You already spend three hours a night on banner colours. Ten minutes on levers decides which three hours count.

## What six more months of guessing cost

Leave the levers unnamed and the numbers stay where they are. Andrew's margin stays at 9%: €2,700 a month instead of the €4,500 a 15% margin would bring. That is €10,800 missing over six months.

His cash reserve reaches its two-month goal at month 16 instead of month 10. Six extra months with no cushion, where one breakdown costs him a full month of progress.

And the owner pays twice. Effort poured into what will not budge returns nothing but fatigue.

## Where the lever list stops

The list treats a symptom: effort spent on things that don't obey you.

The cause is still in place. You know what you can move. You don't yet know which lever goes first, with how much money, and what stays off limits even when it would work. And you learn the month's result only when the month is over.

Two steps close that gap.

- **One page per goal.** Levers in order, resources, written limits, a leading indicator, a deadline and an owner. Chapter 6.
- **Control by forecast.** A weekly reading of where the month will land, so you correct course before it ends. Chapter 9.

Without them, the list is a pile of bricks with no blueprint.

## FAQ

### Which ecommerce mistakes can I fix this week?

Pushing on sales instead of a lever. Pulling three levers on one day, so you can't tell which worked. Checking only monthly revenue. Write your levers, pull one, and read one leading number every week.

### How do I raise my ecommerce margin?

Move the levers that act on it: prices, range, purchasing terms and what each order costs you. Test a price rise in small steps. Andrew's 5% rise lifted his margin from 9% to about 12%.

### Which Etsy shop tips can I use today?

Rewrite the headline of your shop page and change the order of its blocks. Then count visitors and the share who buy, every week. The complete method, from the goal list to the forecast, is in the full control system.
