You can give every euro a job and still run out of money in perfect order.

The budget balances to zero. It doesn't say which job wins when two of them fight.

Picture the first of the month. Every category has its figure. The total reads zero, and it feels like control. Then the milk supplier raises his price. Your best barista asks for a second pair of hands on Fridays. And you still want those loyalty cards.

Three fair requests. One budget. Which line gives way?

The spreadsheet balances to the cent. The cut still happens at midnight, [decided by fatigue](/blog/decision-fatigue-small-business/), the last phone call and whoever asks the loudest.

A budget without an order hands every hard choice to the tired version of you. The Jirgenson Model answers that pain with a control system, and its first step is a list of goals with an order. It is scientifically proven that the Jirgenson Model works ([doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934)).

## What a zero based budget is

A zero based budget gives every unit of income a job before the month starts. Income minus everything assigned equals zero. Nothing floats.

In a company the same name means one more thing. Each budget starts from nothing. Every cost has to justify itself again, instead of last year's figure plus a bit.

Both versions answer one question well: where does each euro go? Neither answers the question that matters on a bad day: which euro goes first when there aren't enough?

Zero says the sum is complete. The order says what survives a bad month.

<div class="def"><strong>In the Jirgenson Model,</strong> a goal list becomes a goal vector once it has an order. The order comes from the refusal test. Give up your goals one at a time: which goes first, and which is the last one left? The last one standing is goal number one.</div>

## The four layers of a zero based budget

[The refusal order](/blog/set-priorities-refusal-order/) tells you what matters more. It doesn't yet tell you what you may never cut. So sort every category into four layers before you assign a single euro.

1. **Constraints.** The law, safety, contracts, wages, taxes. You never "give them up in turn". They define which choices are allowed at all.
2. **Minimum acceptable levels.** The floor below which the result no longer works.
3. **Improvement goals.** These are the ones you rank with the refusal test.
4. **A rule for what remains.** Urgency, risk and cost decide where the rest goes.

If saving the main goal would break a constraint, [the constraint wins](/blog/goal-hierarchy/). A small saving on equipment upkeep brings money today and raises the risk of a breakdown tomorrow. Putting the reserve first doesn't overrule common sense.

## Budget categories template, filled in

Andrew runs a coffee shop called "The Corner". Revenue is €30,000 a month. He wrote five goals, each with a number and a deadline, and then ran the refusal test.

| Rank | Goal | Value | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cash reserve | at least 2.0 months of expenses | month 12 |
| 2 | Margin | 15% | month 6 |
| 3 | Regular customers | 40% of receipts | month 12 |
| 4 | Quality | no more than 2 complaints a week | month 3 |
| 5 | Owner's time | no more than 40 hours a week | month 12 |

What would he defend to the end? Money. Without a reserve the shop closes, and then nothing else on the list matters. He put his own time last, and surprised himself.

Now each budget category gets a layer, the goal it serves and an amount. Here is Andrew's month at today's 9% margin. The table is an illustration built from his figures.

| Layer | Category | Goal it serves | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constraint | Rent, wages and other running costs | Wages and taxes paid on time | €18,300 |
| Constraint and goal 4 | Ingredients | Quality; never cheaper beans | €9,000 |
| Goal 1 | Cash reserve | Reserve of 2.0 months by month 12 | €2,700 |
| Goal 3 | Loyalty cards | Regular customers | €0 while the reserve sits under one month |
| Left over | | | €0 |

€18,300 + €9,000 + €2,700 = €30,000. Zero left. Every euro has a job, and every job has a rank.

Look at the loyalty cards row. Andrew wanted them badly. Cards cost money: printing, discounts, setting up the till. That money would come out of a reserve of half a month. The order said no, so he put the cards aside, and for the first time felt no guilt about it.

## Build your own in fifteen minutes

Andrew wrote his five goals in fifteen minutes. The budget takes one more evening. Here are the steps.

**1. List what you want, then test each item.** Ask of every line: what for? Andrew's rough list had eight items. Three were methods, not goals: loyalty cards, a second barista, a new coffee machine. The cards serve regular customers. The barista and the machine both serve quality. Three methods collapsed into two goals already on the list.

**2. Give every goal a number, a unit and a deadline.** "A good profit" has no number. "15" has no unit. "15%" has no deadline. ["15% by month six" is a goal](/blog/how-to-set-measurable-goals/).

**3. Keep it short.** At most nine goals, from six areas: finance, clients, sales, processes and quality, people, and the owner. [Ten priorities aren't priorities](/blog/too-many-goals/). They're a list.

**4. Write the constraints above the list.** What you will never do, even to save the main goal. Andrew: never cheaper beans, never late wages.

**5. Run the refusal test.** Imagine you have half the resources. Cross goals out one at a time, first to go first. Number them in reverse.

**6. Assign the euros in that order.** Constraints first. Then goal one, goal two, down the list. What can't be funded this month gets €0 and a condition, not a quiet promise.

Then copy the template above and fill it with your own rows.

## Five ways a zero based budget breaks

A budget can balance to zero and still be built badly. The same five defects spoil a goal list.

- **Categories that can't live together.** The lowest prices on the street, the best coffee in town and a 30% margin. Each is possible alone. [Together they're most likely not](/blog/mutually-exclusive-goals/). Test them against your own costs.
- **A missing requirement.** Growth and advertising make the list. "Pay taxes on time" doesn't. That's not a goal to rank. It's a constraint, and it goes above the list.
- **Priorities that change every Monday.** The money never stays in one row long enough to do anything.
- **A broken order.** Andrew buys expensive beans for "quality" while the reserve sits below the safe minimum. A lower goal gets rescued at the cost of the top one.
- **Duplicates.** "Satisfied customers" and "few complaints" under two names. One goal, double weight, twice the money.

## Same goals, two budgets

Here is the contrast, as an illustration.

The usual way: five categories, all "equally important". A week later the supplier raises the milk price. Sara asks for a Friday barista. Andrew agrees, because he is tired and doesn't want to argue. A month later he finds he ran out of money before he ran out of time.

The model's way: the same five goals, in order. The reserve comes first, so the Friday hire and the cards wait. The margin plan feeds the reserve. If that plan works, the margin reaches 15% and monthly costs fall to €25,500. The same €30,000 month then sends €4,500 to the reserve instead of €2,700.

The order did the deciding, not the fatigue.

A household works the same way. Alex is thirteen and [wants a €300 bicycle](/blog/save-for-a-goal/) by 1 September. He ranked his wishes: the bicycle last to give up, football second, games first to go. Pocket money of €20 a month and about €30 from odd jobs all go to one row. If your own household runs short every month, the order of cuts is in [household budget in deficit](/blog/household-budget-deficit/).

## What six months without an order cost

Leave the budget unranked, and the numbers rank it for you.

At Andrew's 9% margin, the reserve grows by about 0.10 months each month. It reaches two months at month 16. With the margin plan working, at month 10. That's six extra months one repair away from trouble.

And every breakdown costs more. At 9%, a repair worth 0.1 months of expenses sets the goal back a full month. At 15%, about half a month.

The human cost is plainer. Every evening, a man tired enough to say yes to whoever asks next.

## What the ordered budget does not fix

Know what you have now. You know where every euro goes, and which goal wins a fight. That treats the symptom: fatigue stops making the choice.

The cause of the next surprise is still there. Your budget rests on an income figure, and nobody has checked it yet. Andrew believed [his margin](/blog/how-to-calculate-profit-margin/) held at around twenty per cent. A budget built on that number would have handed out money that didn't exist. And you still can't tell when a row has drifted far enough to move money out of another.

Two steps close that gap:

- **The measured state, one source per number.** What each row holds today, from the bank statement and the accounts. Never from memory. Chapter 2.
- **Zone thresholds and the red protocol.** [Where normal ends, where an emergency begins](/blog/kpi-zones/), and the order of action when several rows slip at once. Chapter 4.

The ordered budget is the first stage of a closed loop: goal, state, gap, levers, plan, forecast. Each stage has its own tool. The model's canon is open to anyone [on GitHub](https://github.com/mjirgensons/jirgenson-canon).

## FAQ

### What is a zero based budget?

A budget in which every euro of income gets a job, so income minus everything assigned equals zero. In a company it also means building each budget from nothing, with every cost justified again.

### What budget categories should I use?

Start with four layers. Constraints such as wages, taxes and contracts. Minimum levels. Your goals, ranked by the refusal test. A rule for the rest. Group goals by six areas: finance, clients, sales, processes and quality, people, and the owner.

### Is there a budget categories template?

Use four columns: layer, category, the goal it serves, amount. Fill them in order, constraints first, and check that the total reaches zero. The full system that adds measured numbers, zones and forecasts is in [*Steer, Don't Guess*](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HPCJCWKF).
