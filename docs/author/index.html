About the author

Martin Jirgenson

Martin Jirgenson is a researcher of management systems and the author of Steer, Don't Guess (2026). Based in Ontario, Canada, he developed the Jirgenson Model to help owners, managers and families make decisions by instruments rather than by guesswork, with or without AI.

Background

Martin Jirgenson is a researcher in control systems, a management practitioner of many years’ standing and a developer of methods for optimizing business processes. His field lies where strategy meets execution: from goals and measurements to working operational and software tools. Drawing on experience in crisis management, the architecture of complex systems and the fundamental laws of control theory, he has built an integrative model that turns academic rigour into everyday tools for business and life. The Jirgenson Model is the fruit of this work.

The Jirgenson Model

The model treats any result as a process with points of control. Management is three things done regularly: know where you want to go (a short, ordered list of measurable goals), know where you really are (the same list filled with measured facts), and reduce the gap (levers you can move, kept apart from conditions you cannot). The rest of the model, zones and red lines, three schemes of control, three offsets, the backward move, exists to do those three things precisely and on course.
