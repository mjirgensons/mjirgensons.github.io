Day Trading Statistics: Draw Your Red Line Before the Bell

Martin Jirgenson6 Nov 2026 · 10 min read
Short answer

Day trading statistics show the odds for everyone who starts. Your account needs something they can't give: three zones set in advance. Green means leave it alone. Yellow means find the cause while it's cheap. Red means stop everything else and bring the number back. Write the thresholds on a calm evening, and never move them during a losing streak.

Three out of four new day traders quit within two years.

It didn't have to go that way for all of them. Some might have stopped earlier with most of their money, or kept going on firmer ground, had they steered with a control system. With the Jirgenson Model, that number could look different.

It's 2:40 in the afternoon. The account is down for the third day running. Is this a bad week, or the start of the end? Without a line drawn in advance, your mood answers.

The irony is hard to miss. Your platform shows the price to four decimals and paints every tick red or green. Your own account, the one that pays for everything, has no colours at all.

Day trading statistics and the gauge with three sectors

The quitting line hides two different exits. One trader stops at a line chosen in advance, with the account still in one piece. Another stops when the money or the nerve runs out. Give yourself the first kind.

Look at an engine temperature gauge. Needle in the green: drive on. In the yellow: pay closer attention. In the red: stop and let the engine cool. A red light is an order, not a death sentence for the car. The engine is still whole. What matters is to act now.

What to do: give every number you care about three zones, the same way. Normal, acceptable, emergency.

How to avoid an exit by exhaustion: set the red line on a calm evening, long before the afternoon that tests it.

In the Jirgenson Model, the error is the gap between the goal and the state, row by row: actual minus goal. Three zones, set in advance, turn that gap into a signal. The needle shows a sector, not a verdict.

A trading plan template for the zones, filled in

This is the tool. One sheet, about fifteen minutes. Six steps, each with a real entry beside it.

The entries come from two people in the model. Alex, thirteen, is saving €300 for a bicycle by 1 September. Elena is a freelance designer with one large client.

1. Write the gap for each row

Take each goal and its current value. Subtract: actual minus goal, or actual minus where your plan says you should be today.

Alex's entry: by his schedule he should have €170 on 1 July. He has €140. The error is minus €30.

Your rows, as an illustration: the account against the sum you set aside for trading. The loss in one day. Your hours at the screens.

2. Set three zones for each row

Write where normal ends, and where the emergency begins.

Row Normal Acceptable Emergency
Alex: behind schedule up to €10 €11 to €40 more than €40
Elena: largest client's share up to 33% over 33% to 50% over 50%

Alex at minus €30 sits in the yellow. No panic. The cause is clear: his scooter hasn't sold yet. He watches the shortfall closely.

Elena's goal is no more than 30%. Her largest client brings in 70%. That is red.

3. Put the red line where damage turns hard to reverse

The red boundary isn't a guess. It sits where repair gets hard.

Andrew, who runs a coffee shop, set the red line for his cash reserve at one month. A month gives him time to negotiate a deferral, find money and make a decision. With less than a month, there is no time left for any of that.

4. Check each zone against your measurement

A tolerance can't be narrower than the error in your numbers. If your figures can be off by one point, a green zone half a point wide means nothing. Fix the measurement, not the zone.

If a row hovers near a boundary, use one threshold to enter a zone and another to leave it. Or wait for two or three readings in a row. Then the colour won't flicker with every bit of noise.

5. Write what each colour makes you do

  • Green: nothing. Control includes the ability to leave things alone.
  • Yellow: find the cause while that is still cheap. What changed since last time? Since when? Which lever affects it?
  • Red: the red protocol, below. Everything else waits.

6. Rank the reds before two arrive at once

Elena's client share is already red. Then the tax authority freezes her account. Two reds. Income is her goal number one, and the frozen account hits it directly. So she puts the client problem on hold and turns all her attention to the account. The ranking needs your goals in order first; the refusal test in day trading success rate sets it.

That is the whole template. The gap, three zones, a red line where damage begins. A check against the measurement, an action per colour, a rule for two reds. Andrew set his thresholds for five rows in one evening, while his head was clear.

Trading losses and the red protocol

Of every hundred day traders who stuck with it, ninety-seven lost money.

The model gives no trading signals. It can't pick a winning trade. So it can't promise you a winning year. What it can do is show a losing streak as a colour, on a line you drew yourself, while there is still something to save. Some of those traders might have kept more of their money that way.

When a row enters the red zone, a short sequence applies:

  1. Check for a direct threat: to people, to safety, to mandatory payments, to the law. If there is one, protect first.
  2. Freeze secondary work. Anything that doesn't help the red row waits.
  3. Put your time, money and attention on the red row and the levers that act on it.
  4. With several reds, start with the one closest to damage you can't undo. Then the one tied to your highest goal.

This is not ruthlessness. It is economy of effort.

One rule protects the whole system. Thresholds are written in a calm moment and revised rarely. Moving them in the middle of a crisis bends the yardstick to fit the result.

How to avoid that line by default: write the red protocol under your zones tonight. On the red day, you read it. You don't invent it.

Risk of ruin and the stability margin

Every system has a stability margin: how hard a hit it can take without leaving the permissible zone. A broken coffee machine is a nuisance for a shop with two months of reserve. For a shop with two weeks, it is an emergency.

Same machine, same repair bill. Only the margin differs.

So measure the margin, and build it before you need it. That is why Andrew's list holds a cash reserve. In your rows, as an illustration, the margin is the money and the time that sit outside the trading account.

Quit day trading by mood, or by a line

An illustration: one losing week, two traders.

The first has no zones. Every loss feels the same. On Monday he shrugs. By Thursday he can't sleep, and he decides at the worst hour, in the worst mood.

The second has zones. Monday's dip lands in the yellow, so he looks for the cause. Thursday's loss crosses the red line he wrote in May. He follows the protocol. The decision was made weeks ago, by a calmer version of him.

Andrew lived the same fork. His first thought was to launch loyalty cards and win customers back. But the reserve sat in the red, and the cards cost money. Until the red zone closed, everything else was a luxury. He put the cards aside and, for the first time, felt no guilt.

Zones are one piece of a loop: goal, state, error, levers, plan, forecast. They turn the gap into a signal you can act on.

"I already use a stop-loss." Good. A stop-loss guards one position. The zones guard the account, the family money and your hours, all at once, in an order you chose.

The Jirgenson Model is scientifically proven to work (doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23964934). It is new, so it came too late for the traders behind these numbers. It can help you read your own account now.

What a gap without zones costs

Andrew's numbers show it in money. His reserve stood at 0.5 months against a goal of 2.0. The error, minus 1.5 months, meant a hole of €40,950 at €27,300 of monthly expenses.

His margin stood 6 points below the goal. On €30,000 of revenue, that is €1,800 a month: €2,700 of profit instead of €4,500. Six more months without a signal is €10,800 never earned.

Without zones, two such gaps read as one vague "tough period". With zones, the reserve showed red and the margin yellow, and the order of work followed.

The human cost is plain. Every loss feels like the same emergency, so every evening ends in front of the screens.

What the zones do not fix

The zones tell you when to act and what comes first. They treat a symptom: you stop deciding by mood.

The cause of a losing year can stay in place. You still don't know which moves are yours to make and which belong to the market. And you have no one-page plan for bringing a red row back.

Two steps close that gap:

  • Levers. The short list of things you control yourself, apart from the ones you can't, so you stop scolding the ice. Chapter 5.
  • The control concept. One page per goal: the levers, the resources, the constraints you won't break even to save the goal. Chapter 6.

Without them, the zones tell you the needle is red. They don't tell you which wheel to turn.

Key takeaways

  • The error is actual minus goal, row by row.
  • Set three zones per row on a calm evening. Put the red line where damage turns hard to reverse.
  • Green: leave it alone. Yellow: find the cause. Red: follow the protocol.
  • Rank the reds before two arrive at once.
  • Never move a threshold in the middle of a crisis.

Before the next red afternoon

Two roads are open tonight. Keep trading by feel, and let the third red day decide for you. Or take fifteen minutes and draw three zones under every number that matters.

The zones tell you when. The levers and the one-page plan tell you what to move and how.

Questions people ask

What do day trading statistics mean for me?

They give the odds for everyone who starts. They can't tell you where your own account stands. Three zones on your own rows can: normal, acceptable or emergency, on lines you set in advance.

What should a trading plan template include for risk?

The gap for each row and three zones per row. A red line where damage gets hard to reverse. An action for each colour, and a rule for two reds at once. It gives no trading signals.

How do I know it's time to quit day trading?

Write the stop condition on a calm evening, as a number with a date. The zones show how close you are to it. The book also covers when to change the plan and when to change the goal itself.
