The story

A management method was written down as a compact rulebook (a “canon”) and an operating prompt, so that an AI assistant works by the method instead of giving generic advice. It was then blind-tested: the same model answered ten realistic small-business cases with and without the method, judged by a stronger model against a pre-registered answer key. With the method the score was 88.9 out of 100 against 55.9 without; the judge preferred the method’s answer in 30 of 30 pairs; the share of answers containing an invented fact fell from 75% to 11%. The limits: one judge model, one model family, ten cases. Method, data and judgments are public.

The method is the Jirgenson Model, published in the book Steer, Don’t Guess (October 2026). The author is Martin Jirgenson, a management systems researcher based in Ontario, Canada.

Author bio

Short. Martin Jirgenson is a researcher of management systems and the author of Steer, Don’t Guess (2026). Based in Ontario, Canada, he developed the Jirgenson Model to help owners, managers and families make decisions by instruments rather than by guesswork, with or without AI.

Long. Martin Jirgenson is a researcher in control systems, a management practitioner of many years’ standing and a developer of methods for optimizing business processes. His field lies where strategy meets execution: from goals and measurements to working operational and software tools. Drawing on experience in crisis management, the architecture of complex systems and the fundamental laws of control theory, he has built an integrative model that turns academic rigour into everyday tools for business and life. The Jirgenson Model is the fruit of this work.
